How much the planet has warmed

What this shows. The difference between each year's global average surface temperature and the 1951 to 1980 average, in degrees Celsius. Two independent teams, NASA and NOAA, measure this separately. They agree closely, which is a useful thing to point out during a demonstration: the trend is not one group's opinion.

Rainfall in India

Total rainfall each year

Typical rainfall by month

What this shows. Rainfall in millimetres for one of the 36 meteorological sub-divisions of India, from 1901 to 2015. The thick line is a ten-year average, which matters because rainfall swings a lot from one year to the next. A single dry year is weather. A shift in the ten-year line is closer to climate.

Carbon dioxide emissions

Where the selected country's emissions come from

What this shows. Annual carbon dioxide released from fossil fuels and industry, in million tonnes. The lower chart splits it by fuel, not by end use, so it answers "which fuel" rather than "which sector". Land use change is shown separately because it is measured with more uncertainty than fuel burning.

Country total

Per person living there

What this shows. The same year, ranked two different ways. Large countries dominate the left chart simply because many people live there. The right chart divides by population and the order changes completely. Showing both is the honest way to present this comparison, and it is usually the moment an audience starts asking questions.

Share of electricity generated from renewable sources

What this shows. The percentage of each country's electricity that came from hydro, wind, solar and other renewable sources. This covers electricity only, not transport, cooking or industrial heat, so a high figure here does not mean a country has stopped using fossil fuels.

Where the data comes from

  • Temperature: NASA GISTEMP and NOAA GCAG annual global anomalies, 1880 to 2025.
  • Emissions and population: Our World in Data, owid-co2-data, compiled from the Global Carbon Budget. Latest complete year 2024.
  • Electricity mix: Our World in Data, owid-energy-data, compiled from Ember and the Energy Institute.
  • Rainfall: India Meteorological Department sub-divisional monthly rainfall, 1901 to 2015. The all-India series here is a plain average of the 36 sub-divisions and is not weighted by area.

Data retrieved 20 September 2026. This dashboard is built for education and awareness. It is not a forecasting tool, and rainfall records end in 2015, so it cannot describe the last decade of Indian monsoons.
